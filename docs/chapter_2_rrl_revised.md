# REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE

Transportation in small municipalities continues to depend on flexible and semi-formal modes such as tricycles, especially in communities where fixed-route public transportation is limited. In the Philippine context, tricycles support short-distance mobility, access to schools, markets, terminals, and residential areas, and local livelihood for drivers. However, manual tricycle operations can also result in uncertain passenger waiting time, limited trip visibility, inconsistent service quality, weak driver-passenger accountability, and difficulty in monitoring active trips. Since SakayNow Buenatoda is designed as a geofencing-based tricycle booking system with real-time tracking, role-based navigation, account verification, fare visibility, and feedback features, this review begins with literature on geofencing and location-based transport systems, then proceeds to ride-hailing and first-mile/last-mile mobility, Philippine public transport service quality, tricycle and motorcycle taxi studies, mobile application feasibility, and the research gap addressed by the proposed system.

Geofencing is a location-based technology that defines virtual boundaries and enables a system to respond when a device, vehicle, or user enters, exits, or remains within a defined area. In transport systems, geofencing is useful because it connects physical movement with digital rules, monitoring, and service control. Nait-Sidi-Moh et al. (2013) explained that location-based services and geofencing can support road safety and transport efficiency by enabling real-time tracking and information exchange among transport actors. Their study is relevant to SakayNow Buenatoda because the proposed system depends on live location data, driver-passenger proximity, pickup radius checking, and trip monitoring.

More recent work also shows that geofencing is not limited to simple tracking. Hansen et al. (2024) described geofencing as a tool for defining digital zones and dynamic mobility rules in cities. Their discussion of geofencing for transport management supports the idea that a local transport application can use geographic boundaries to keep service operations within an authorized or intended area. This is important for SakayNow Buenatoda because tricycle operations in Buenavista are local by nature and are expected to function within municipal service areas. A geofenced approach can therefore help align the system with local dispatching, monitoring, and operational control.

Ride-hailing literature provides the broader platform background for SakayNow Buenatoda. Tirachini (2020) reviewed international evidence on ride-hailing and found that app-based services affect mobility access, travel behavior, modal choice, and transport regulation. While ride-hailing can improve comfort, security, and matching efficiency, it also raises questions about public transport substitution, congestion, and governance. This literature is useful because SakayNow Buenatoda does not merely transfer manual tricycle hailing into a digital form; it introduces a platform-based process where passengers request rides, drivers receive demand, and service records become visible to both users and administrators.

In relation to first-mile and last-mile mobility, Sunitiyoso et al. (2022) found that ride-hailing can complement mass transport by helping commuters complete the beginning or end portions of their journeys. Their study of multimodal commuting in Greater Jakarta showed that ride-hailing is especially useful when access to stations or stops is beyond walking distance or when feeder services are unreliable. This finding relates to the local function of tricycles in Philippine municipalities, where tricycles often connect passengers from homes, schools, and barangays to central destinations. SakayNow Buenatoda can be viewed as a localized first-mile and last-mile tool because it digitizes short-distance access to tricycle transport.

Motorcycle ride-hailing research further supports the role of small vehicles in connecting users to broader transport systems. Thaithatkul et al. (2023) compared car and motorcycle ride-hailing use in Bangkok and found that motorcycle ride-hailing was used more frequently and was significantly connected to urban rail access. Although the setting is Thailand, the study is relevant because motorcycles and motorcycle-based public transport are commonly used in Southeast Asian cities for short trips, narrow roads, and flexible access. This supports SakayNow Buenatoda's focus on tricycles as a practical local mobility mode rather than a full substitute for larger public transport.

Service quality and user satisfaction are also central to app-based transport systems. Shah and Kubota (2022) studied app-based ride-hailing services in Lahore, Pakistan, and found that service quality attributes influence passenger satisfaction and future usage intention. Their study emphasized the role of system attributes, reliability, professionalism, and service attraction in sustaining user trust. These findings justify SakayNow Buenatoda's inclusion of driver information, trip records, role-based interfaces, and feedback mechanisms because user satisfaction depends not only on booking availability but also on service reliability and perceived safety.

Philippine public transport literature provides a stronger local foundation for the proposed system. Chuenyindee et al. (2022) examined public utility vehicle service quality and customer satisfaction in the Philippines during the COVID-19 pandemic. Their study showed that service quality dimensions such as tangibility, assurance, and safety-related practices influence customer satisfaction. Although the study covers public utility vehicles broadly, it supports the importance of cleanliness, safety assurance, and orderly service in local transport. For SakayNow Buenatoda, these findings support features such as account verification, driver monitoring, admin approval, and transparent ride records.

Tricycle-specific evidence is especially important because SakayNow Buenatoda is not a general ride-hailing application. Ong et al. (2023) studied tricycle service quality and passenger satisfaction in Philippine urban areas using the SERVQUAL approach. Their findings showed that responsiveness, tangibles, empathy, and passenger satisfaction significantly affect the overall evaluation of tricycle services. This study directly supports the proposed system because SakayNow Buenatoda addresses responsiveness through digital booking and driver queue management, tangibles through verified user and driver records, and satisfaction through trip history and feedback features.

Related Philippine motorcycle taxi research also supports the importance of trust, accessibility, and service quality in two- and three-wheeled transport. Ong et al. (2024) examined motorcycle taxi transportation in the Philippines using SERVQUAL dimensions and social exchange theory. Their study found that trust, economic benefit, traffic management, accessibility, tangibles, empathy, reliability, and assurance were important to service quality and customer satisfaction. Although motorcycle taxis are different from tricycles, the study is useful because both modes involve app-based or flexible short-distance transport where passenger trust, driver behavior, safety, and accessibility strongly influence continued use.

The livelihood and operational role of tricycle drivers must also be considered. Yamano et al. (2022) studied post-pandemic employment recovery among tricycle drivers in Metro Manila using panel data from drivers surveyed in 2019 and 2021. The study showed that tricycle drivers experienced severe income disruption during the pandemic and that their recovery depended on the return of transport demand, clear regulations, and safer public transport operations. This supports the social relevance of SakayNow Buenatoda because the system does not only benefit passengers; it can also help drivers by improving access to passenger demand, reducing idle waiting, and creating clearer trip records.

Application-based studies show that mobile systems are feasible for transport-related services when they are designed around user needs, real-time information, and accessibility. Ulay et al. (2025) developed GTRIKE, an internet-based mobile application for tricycle reservation in San Jose, Occidental Mindoro. The study used Flutter, geolocation, and Firebase, which are closely related to SakayNow Buenatoda's architecture. It demonstrated that a tricycle reservation application can provide real-time driver and passenger availability updates and can function under different network conditions. However, its evaluation involved only 15 respondents, which means it is useful as a feasibility reference but should be supported by broader transport and service quality studies.

Taken together, the literature shows that geofencing can support location-aware transport control, ride-hailing can improve access and matching efficiency, and service quality strongly influences passenger satisfaction in app-based and Philippine public transport settings. The most directly related studies confirm that tricycles remain important in Philippine mobility and that digital tricycle reservation is technically feasible. However, the literature also shows that many systems and studies focus on broader ride-hailing, bus commuting, motorcycle taxis, or urban public transport rather than a municipal tricycle booking system tailored to a specific locality.

## Research Gap

Existing studies provide strong support for the individual components of SakayNow Buenatoda, including geofencing, ride-hailing, service quality, user satisfaction, and mobile transport applications. However, there remains a gap in localized systems that combine these components into one role-based tricycle booking platform for a small municipality. Prior studies either focus on general ride-hailing behavior, motorcycle taxi services, public utility vehicle satisfaction, or prototype tricycle reservation systems with limited evaluation samples. Few studies specifically address a geofencing-based tricycle booking system that integrates passenger booking, driver availability, real-time tracking, account verification, admin approval, trip monitoring, saved locations, fare visibility, and feedback in one Firebase-backed mobile application.

SakayNow Buenatoda addresses this gap by applying geofencing, real-time location tracking, role-based navigation, Firebase cloud services, and service quality principles to the local tricycle context of Buenavista, Bohol. The proposed system is intended to improve passenger convenience, driver-passenger matching, driver accountability, admin monitoring, and transparent ride records while preserving the local function of tricycles as an accessible short-distance transport mode.
