# Chapter 2 RRL Revision Notes

## Removed or Replaced Studies

- Patel et al. (2022) - Removed because the citation in the original Chapter 2 was too vague. The study title and source were not clearly identified, and the statement about mobility-on-demand features can be supported more strongly by traceable ride-hailing and first-mile/last-mile studies.

- Tran et al. (2024) - Removed because the original Chapter 2 did not provide enough citation detail to verify the exact source. Its feeder-transport point was replaced with stronger, traceable evidence from Sunitiyoso et al. (2022) and Thaithatkul et al. (2023).

- Martin et al. (2023) - Removed from the revised draft because the study was broad and focused on motorcycle taxi digitalization outside the Philippine tricycle context. The revised Chapter 2 instead uses Philippine tricycle, Philippine motorcycle taxi, and Southeast Asian ride-hailing studies with clearer links to SakayNow.

- Alvarez et al. (2024) BUSabay - Removed because it focuses on bus commuting rather than tricycle booking. It is real and useful for commuter app usability, but it is weaker than GTRIKE as evidence for SakayNow's tricycle booking scope.

## Added or Strengthened Studies

- Nait-Sidi-Moh et al. (2013) - Supports location-based services, geofencing, real-time tracking, road safety, and transport efficiency.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-03737-0_14

- Hansen et al. (2024) - Supports geofenced service areas, digital mobility zones, and transport management.
  Link: https://westminsterresearch.westminster.ac.uk/item/w840x/geofencing-to-accelerate-digital-transitions-in-cities-experiences-and-findings-from-the-geosence-project

- Tirachini (2020) - Supports broader ride-hailing effects on access, travel behavior, regulation, and platform-based transport.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11116-019-10070-2

- Sunitiyoso et al. (2022) - Supports ride-hailing as first-mile/last-mile and feeder mobility.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cstp.2022.04.013

- Thaithatkul et al. (2023) - Supports motorcycle ride-hailing as a connector to public transport in Southeast Asia.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cstp.2023.100950

- Shah and Kubota (2022) - Supports app-based ride-hailing service quality, satisfaction, and continued usage intention.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eastsj.2022.100076

- Chuenyindee et al. (2022) - Supports Philippine PUV service quality, assurance, tangibility, and customer satisfaction.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jup.2022.101336

- Ong et al. (2023) - Provides direct Philippine tricycle service quality and satisfaction evidence using SERVQUAL.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cities.2023.104339

- Ong et al. (2024) - Supports Philippine motorcycle taxi service quality, trust, accessibility, and satisfaction.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cstp.2023.101139

- Ulay et al. (2025) - Provides the closest direct tricycle booking application reference, using Flutter, geolocation, and Firebase.
  Link: https://doi.org/10.5861/ijrset.2025.25012

- Yamano et al. (2022) - Supports the social and livelihood importance of Philippine tricycle drivers.
  Link: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/821046/adbi-informality-book-web.pdf

## Added Research Gap

The revised Chapter 2 now states that previous studies support individual components of SakayNow Buenatoda, but few combine geofencing, passenger booking, driver availability, real-time tracking, account verification, admin approval, fare visibility, saved locations, and feedback into one Firebase-backed municipal tricycle booking system.
